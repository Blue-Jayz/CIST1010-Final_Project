Study Smarter: Environment, Tools & Ethics

Studying more hours does not guarantee better grades. The students who thrive in college study in environments optimized for silence, leverage modern tools efficiently, and strictly adhere to academic integrity.

1. Finding Your Spot: The 3rd Floor of Criss Library

During my first week or two on campus, I tried studying all over the place. I tried sitting in the Milo Bail Student Center, but the background chatter and food court foot traffic constantly broke my train of thought. I tried studying at home in my bedroom, but my bed, television, and refrigerator were constant temptations.

Then I explored UNO Criss Library and discovered the 3rd floor.

Unlike the 1st floor (which is lively and collaborative), the 3rd floor is designated as a silent study zone. It is nearly silent. The atmosphere is filled with students deeply immersed in reading, writing, and coding. Being surrounded by people who are all locked into their work creates positive peer pressure.

Through this trial and error, I learned three crucial truths about my learning style:

  • I study best alone: Group study is great for review, but initial mastery requires deep personal focus.
  • I need absolute quiet: White noise headphones or silent rooms let my brain process tough STEM equations without audio friction.
  • I must study on campus: As a commuter, staying on campus creates a psychological boundary between "work time" at UNO and "rest time" at home. When I pack up my backpack and drive home, my homework is already done.

Criss Library 3rd Floor Tip: Head toward the north and east window perimeters. The individual study carrels provide natural lighting, direct power outlets for your laptop and phone charger, and a clear line of sight overlooking campus trees while maintaining strict quiet-floor courtesy.

Where I Learned This (Lesson 4) Source: Personal Experience

Specific Source: Trying several study spots during my first 1 to 2 weeks at UNO.
What it taught me: I study best alone, in a quiet place, and on campus rather than at home.

Why This Matters The Power of Environmental Cues

Your physical environment acts as an automatic trigger for your mental state. If you try to study on your bed, your brain associates that space with relaxation and sleep, resulting in sluggish thinking. By establishing a dedicated, sacred study sanctuary on the 3rd floor of Criss Library, simply sitting in that chair instantly cues your mind to lock in, saving mental energy and shortening the time required to understand complex material.

2. Modern Note-Taking: Note Cast & NotebookLM

In college lectures, professors often speak quickly and cover dozens of slides in a single 75-minute period. If you spend every second frantically typing down every word they say, you aren't actually listening or understanding the concepts.

To study more efficiently, I integrated two modern software tools into my workflow:

🎧

Note Cast

I use Note Cast for lecture transcription and quick audio note generation. It catches anything I might have missed during fast explanations, allowing me to focus on participating and understanding during class rather than typing blindly.

📝

NotebookLM

I use NotebookLM to generate custom practice questions, summarize complex readings, and synthesize organized study guides. It is especially powerful for preparing physical, printed reference sheets for tests that allow paper notes.

The core philosophy behind using these tools is simple: spend more time studying your notes instead of making them.

Critical Policy Check Always Check the Course Syllabus First

Before using any lecture recording, transcription tool, or generative AI assistant, always verify each instructor's individual policy in their course syllabus. Academic policies on AI and recording vary widely across departments at UNO: some professors encourage responsible AI usage, while others strictly prohibit recording devices or AI assistance. Never assume: read the syllabus and confirm with your instructor.

Where I Learned This (Lesson 7) Source: Outside Tools & Course Syllabi

Specific Source: Note Cast (notecast.net) and NotebookLM; each instructor's syllabus for recording and AI policies.
What it taught me: How to make notes much more easily, so I spend more time studying notes instead of making them. AI is the future, so we might as well learn to use it responsibly now.

Why This Matters Responsible Fluency & Higher-Order Thinking

AI is reshaping the technological landscape, and entering the workforce requires knowing how to use intelligent tools ethically and productively rather than fearing them. However, tools must augment your thinking, not replace it. By using AI to automate the administrative friction of formatting notes, you free up cognitive space for higher-order synthesis, problem solving, and genuine conceptual mastery.

Reflect on Your Own Are You Preparing to Study, or Actually Learning?

"Are you spending more time preparing to study (color-coding pens, retyping slides) than actually practicing problems and testing your recall?"

Retyping notes feels productive because your hands are moving, but cognitive research shows it is one of the lowest-retention study methods available.

Click for a high-impact study test

Close your notes right now. On a blank piece of paper, can you explain the core concept from your last lecture in your own words, without looking? If you can't, no amount of pretty highlighting will save you on exam day. Shift your time from decorating notes to active self-testing.
