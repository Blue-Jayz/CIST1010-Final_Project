UNO Resources: The Assets You Already Pay For

Campus Support Deep Dive • Academic support, faculty access, and career pipelines.

Your tuition covers world-class academic support, faculty mentorship, specialized facilities, and professional career pipelines. Don't leave them on the table out of hesitation or fear.

1. Instructors & Office Hours: The #1 Overlooked Resource

When I arrived at UNO, I viewed professors as intimidating authorities. I pictured strict academics who would judge me for asking basic questions or struggling on an assignment.

In CIST 1010 (College and Career Success), we spent time discussing campus support systems and how college faculty operate. That discussion completely shattered my misconception.

The truth is, UNO instructors are remarkably approachable, laid-back, and genuinely passionate about helping students understand the material. They sit in their offices during scheduled office hours waiting for students to drop by. When you visit them, they aren't evaluating your intelligence; they are thrilled that you care enough to seek clarity.

Even if you feel shy about walking into an office, you can start small: stay for 2 to 3 minutes right after lecture ends. I regularly walk up to the podium after class to ask quick clarifying questions about a coding syntax error or a calculus proof. Getting that immediate 2-minute answer prevents hours of confused spinning later that evening.

Finding your instructor's office schedule: Office hours, meeting locations, and communication preferences are listed individually per instructor directly on their course syllabus on Canvas.

Where I Learned This (Lesson 5) Source: Class Instruction

Specific Source: CIST 1010 (College and Career Success).
What it taught me: Not to be afraid of my instructors. They aren't scary, and they genuinely want students to understand the material. I often ask questions right after class to get a better understanding.

Why This Matters Self-Advocacy & Academic Relationships

College is where you learn that asking for help is not a sign of weakness; it is the ultimate professional superpower. Instructors write recommendation letters, connect students to undergraduate research positions, and refer candidates for high-paying industry internships. Taking the initiative to introduce yourself and seek guidance transforms you from an anonymous face in a 60-person lecture hall into an engaged, memorable student.

2. UNO Criss Library

Located in the center of Dodge Campus, the UNO Criss Library is much more than a collection of books:

  • Zoned Quiet Floors: Head directly to the 3rd floor for near-absolute silence and distraction-free study cubicles.
  • Reserve Study Rooms: Book private group study rooms equipped with whiteboards and collaborative display screens for group presentations.
  • Research Assistance & Tech Lending: Access high-end desktop workstations, specialized databases, interlibrary loans, and technical hardware lending.

3. MavSync: Campus Engagement Hub

The MavSync Student Engagement Portal is your gateway to over 150 student organizations, Greek life, campus recreation, and cultural events.

As a commuter, it is easy to feel disconnected from campus life. MavSync allows you to filter meetings and workshops by time and location so you can easily fit club activities around your work shifts and class schedule.

4. College of IS&T Academic Tutoring

If you are taking computer science, cybersecurity, IT innovation, or management information systems, the College of IS&T Student Tutoring Center in the Peter Kiewit Institute (PKI) offers free, walk-in peer tutoring.

Honest Peer Reflection: I have not needed to use the tutoring center personally yet because staying on top of daily coding practice in CSCI 1620 kept me in good shape. However, I know several classmates who credit IS&T tutors with saving their grades on complex data structure algorithms. It is an invaluable resource that every incoming freshman should try the moment a concept feels fuzzy.

5. The UNO Career & Internship Fair

Held every semester, the UNO Career and Internship Fair brings dozens of regional and national employers directly to campus.

Many first-year students make the mistake of thinking: "I'm just a freshman; career fairs are only for seniors." That assumption is completely wrong. Attending the fair as a first-year student gave me the exact opportunity that led to multiple interviews, including one with Union Pacific Railroad. Meeting recruiters face-to-face establishes a human connection that an online resume submission can never replicate.
