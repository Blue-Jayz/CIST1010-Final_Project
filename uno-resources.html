UNO Resources: Support You Already Pay For

Campus Support Deep Dive • Free help, study spaces, and career events already included in your tuition.

Your tuition already covers tutoring, study spaces, instructor office hours, and career fairs. Don't leave them unused just because it feels awkward to ask.

1. Instructors & Office Hours: Don't Be Afraid to Ask

When I first got to UNO, I saw professors as intimidating. I pictured strict teachers who'd judge me for asking basic questions or getting stuck on homework.

In CIST 1010 (College and Career Success), we spent time talking about campus support and how college instructors actually work. That completely changed how I looked at it.

The truth is, UNO instructors are fairly laid-back, and most genuinely want you to understand the material. They sit in their offices during office hours hoping students will stop by. Asking questions doesn't make you look dumb; it shows them you actually care about learning what you're going over.

Even if walking into an office feels awkward at first, start small: stay for 2 to 3 minutes right after class ends. I walk up after lecture all the time to ask a quick clarifying question about a coding bug or a calculus step. Getting that two-minute answer saves hours of spinning my wheels later that night.

Finding your instructor's office schedule: Office hours, office locations, and how they prefer to be reached are posted individually on each course syllabus in Canvas.

Where I Learned This (Lesson 5) Source: Class Instruction

Specific Source: CIST 1010 (College and Career Success).
What it taught me: Not to be afraid of my instructors; it's not like they're really all that scary to begin with, and they genuinely want you to understand the topics you're reviewing. I often ask questions right after class to get a better understanding.

Why This Matters Speaking Up & Building Real Connections

College is where you learn that asking for help isn't a sign of weakness; it's one of the smartest habits you can build. Instructors write recommendation letters, connect students to undergraduate research, and point people toward good internships. Taking a minute to introduce yourself and ask honest questions turns you from an anonymous face in a 60-person lecture into a student they actually know.

2. UNO Criss Library

Located right in the center of Dodge Campus, UNO Criss Library is much more than a building full of books:

  • Quiet Floors: Head straight to the 3rd floor for real silence and individual study desks.
  • Reserve Study Rooms: Book private group study rooms with whiteboards and screens when you need to prep for group projects.
  • Research Help & Tech Lending: Use campus desktop computers, research databases, interlibrary loans, and borrow hardware when you need it.

3. MavSync: Campus Engagement Hub

The MavSync Student Engagement Portal connects you to over 150 student organizations, Greek life, campus rec, and campus events.

As a commuter, it's easy to feel disconnected from campus life. MavSync lets you filter meetings and workshops by day and time, so you can fit club activities around your job and class schedule.

4. College of IS&T Academic Tutoring

If you're taking computer science, cybersecurity, IT innovation, or management information systems, the College of IS&T Student Tutoring Center in the Peter Kiewit Institute (PKI) offers free, walk-in peer tutoring.

Honest peer note: I haven't needed to use the tutoring center myself yet because staying on top of daily coding practice in CSCI 1620 kept me in good shape. Still, I know several classmates who say IS&T tutors saved their grades on tough data-structure topics. It's a great resource to try the moment a concept starts feeling fuzzy.

5. The UNO Career & Internship Fair

Held every semester, the UNO Career and Internship Fair brings dozens of local and national employers directly to campus.

A lot of first-year students make the mistake of thinking, "I'm just a freshman; career fairs are only for seniors." That's simply wrong. Going to the fair as a first-year student led directly to multiple interviews for me, including one with Union Pacific Railroad. Meeting recruiters face-to-face builds a human connection that an online resume submission can't match.
